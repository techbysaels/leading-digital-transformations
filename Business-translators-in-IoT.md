# Business translators in IoT

IoT solutions have the potential to generate large amounts of data that can help companies improve their operations, reduce costs, and make better decisions. However, having access to this information does not necessarily mean that companies know how to use it to generate business value. In many projects, there is a gap between what the customer expects to achieve and what the technical teams understand they need to develop. This gap can lead to solutions that work correctly from a technical perspective but do not fully address the business problem.

In this context, I believe that the role of a business translator becomes particularly important for companies providing IoT solutions. By understanding both the business objectives and the technical possibilities of data and analytics, a business translator can help ensure that the information collected by IoT devices is transformed into useful insights and concrete actions. Integrating this role into the organization could improve communication between teams, support better decisions throughout the project, and ultimately increase the value that customers obtain from the implemented solutions.

## How can the role of a business translator be effectively implemented and integrated into an organziation that specializes in providing IoT solutions to bridge the gap between the business world and the data world?

In that context, I believe a business translator would work with the customer, the project managers, and the technical teams to facilitate the communication between them, as a bridge connecting the different perspectives on a project. Normally, the customers describe their needs and issues in business terms, while developers and data scientists need that information translated to measurable variables, system requirements, and analytical questions.

The business translator should work from the beginning of the project, participating in discovery sessions with the customers to identify the business goals and metrics. Then they would convert them into questions that can be answered by the data. For example, “reduce vehicle maintenance cost” could be translated into “How frequently do unexpected failures occur?” or “Which sensor variables appear before a failure?” that could be transformed into metrics of maintenance cost per vehicle, number of unplanned failures, or prediction accuracy.

They could also be in charge of transforming customer engagement into concrete metrics to monitor. For example, if a customer wants to improve worker safety using IoT devices, measurable indicators should be defined by the translator working closely with the customer. These indicators could be response time to emergency alerts, frequency of unsafe conditions detected, or number of workers entering restricted areas. These metrics would be measured before and after the implementation, allowing the technical team to compare them and see if the project is working for the business objectives or not. 

In IoT projects, business constraints are important because the systems are constantly interacting with the environment and the business, making outcomes applicable to the real process. For instance, predictive maintenance could be triggered by the model when a high probability of failure is detected; however, it can’t be immediately applied. To apply it successfully, business constraints such as technician availability, replacement part availability, vehicle schedules, and the business cost of taking an asset out of operation should be included as variables to consider.

I also consider that a translator would be key to converting the analytical outcomes into business insights and actions understandable by the users. For example, a prediction that has an outcome of 85% probability of asset failure is not understandable or applicable directly to the business; however, from this outcome and the sensor data available, a recommended action could be to inspect the cooling system within the next 48 hours because the probability of failure has exceeded the threshold. In this example, the job of the translator would be to bridge the gap between the outcome as is and a new recommended action that was not possible before because of the gap in data.

## How will hiring a business translator help to ensure successful implementation of intelligent processes and systems using data and analytics?

The business translator would improve the probability of a successful implementation because it will transform the technical possibilities into business decisions, which is, most of the time, a difficult task to manage in IoT solutions.

As IoT projects frequently involve compromises among cost, accuracy, response time, infrastructure, and functionality, the translator must correctly identify the trade-offs to reach the efficient frontier without surpassing it. For example, the frequency of data collection by the sensors could be tuned with different values, and as the frequency is higher, the amount of information is also higher, but the costs also increase. In this scenario, the business translator could help to determine if the additional accuracy provides enough value to justify the increased costs. To balance both, the final solution is normally to collect critical information continuously while transmitting less important or rarely changed variables every few minutes.

Another clear advantage is the improvement in communication between teams and the efficiency within those teams. Business translators would be in charge of taking the requirements of the customers and the stakeholders to transform them into technical requirements for the data science or development teams. Then, misunderstandings would be less, and the teams would be more focused on their tasks instead of going to meetings to clarify them. On the other hand, the customers and stakeholders would concentrate on finding new ways to increase the value of the solution for the business instead of translating the requirements into technical terminology for the understanding of the technical team.
