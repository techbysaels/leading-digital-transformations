### <span style="color:#A31F34;">Step 4: Analyze the strengths and weaknesses of your organization</span>

<p>• <span style="color:#221F1F;">Examine your potential initiatives for clues on what your organization is currently doing and where it will need help. Consider your environment and competition to assess your advantages and disadvantages. </span></p>

<p>• <span style="color:#221F1F;">What are the existing strengths that your digital strategy will build on? </span></p>

<p>• <span style="color:#221F1F;">What does your organization currently lack and need to achieve?</span></p>

<table style="border-collapse:collapse;width:100%;">
<tr><td style="border:1px solid #b8b8b8;padding:8px;vertical-align:top;"><p>To obtain our strengths and principal improvements, I have checked the external perspective and realized that the IoT market is becoming increasingly competitive as cloud providers and other specialized IoT companies incorporate AI and low-code capabilities into their solutions.</p><p>Then, our strengths rely on the knowledge we have gathered from our previous IoT projects. The following are the principal identified strengths that my digital strategy will build on:</p><p>• Experience in integrating sensors with business applications using cloud or on-premises servers.</p><p>• Use of the low-code platform Oracle APEX to develop the IoT platform and customized solutions.</p><p>• Good relationships with customers because of the satisfaction with the solutions developed by the technical teams and follow-up done by the business team.</p><p>• Strong experience in IoT architecture and software development that can be used as historical data.</p><p>However, we need to improve our processes to stay at the top of the market because larger competitors may have greater access to AI infrastructure, reusable products, and development resources. Then, these are the principal improvements discovered that would help us stay in the race:</p><p>• Stronger data analytics and AI capabilities in terms of infrastructure and team knowledge.</p><p>• Better coordination between technical and business teams because we do not share a centralized platform to coordinate the projects.</p><p>• Standardization between projects, including schedule and cost estimations, to develop a framework to be followed by the project managers.</p><p>• Knowledge from previous projects is not reused efficiently because similar requirements are rebuilt instead of using the historical data available.</p></td></tr>
</table>

### <span style="color:#A31F34;">Step 5: Define strategic goals for digital transformation</span>

<p><span style="color:#221F1F;">Based on the analysis above, what are your strategic goals for digital transformation?</span></p>

<table style="border-collapse:collapse;width:100%;">
<tr><td style="border:1px solid #b8b8b8;padding:8px;vertical-align:top;"><p>The goals of my organization for digital transformation would be the following:</p><p>• Expand the centralized IoT platform to at least two new industries within the next three years, developing at least one validated use case for each industry while reusing the existing platform architecture whenever possible.</p><p>• Develop and validate at least three new analytics or AI-enabled capabilities within the next three years, such as predictive maintenance, anomaly detection, or chatbots, and deploy at least two of them in real customer projects.</p><p>• Reduce the average implementation time of comparable new IoT projects by approximately 30% within three years by improving the technical team&#x27;s knowledge of Oracle APEX and using AI-assisted infrastructure and code recommendations based on historical solutions.</p><p>• Implement a centralized project collaboration platform within the next 12 months and have at least 90% of active projects use it to manage requirements, activity progress, responsibilities, and expected completion dates.</p></td></tr>
</table>

<p><span style="color:#221F1F;">Does your organization need short-term digital strategies? Explain why or why not.</span></p>

<table style="border-collapse:collapse;width:100%;">
<tr><td style="border:1px solid #b8b8b8;padding:8px;vertical-align:top;"><p>Yes, the organization will need short-term digital strategies because to reach every one of the goals stated in the previous section, we require a long time to have the solutions developed. As we cannot wait until that moment for new projects, we need to establish short-term strategies. </p><p>For example, to reach the goal “Test and implement analytics and predictive capabilities”, we need to divide it into short-term goals, focusing on one solution at a time that we could provide to one specific customer, like predictive maintenance for mining equipment or crop disease detection for agriculture solutions.</p><p>Another example is “Improve collaboration between business and technical teams” because it depends on little goals such as implementation of a centralized platform for activity progress, prediction of expected finalization date, and cultural change from presenting only the results to presenting iterative results.</p><p>For the “AI-assisted infrastructure and code recommendations” initiative, it&#x27;s also needed because the AI tool should first be tested in a limited number of projects before being adopted across the company.</p><p>For my initiative, the short-term strategic goal proposed would be that during the next 6 months, the organization will pilot the AI-assisted recommendation tool in 3-5 new IoT projects. The objective of the pilot will be to reduce the time spent on repetitive code development, infrastructure design, and architecture definition by at least 20% compared to similar previous projects. The rationale behind it is that it will allow the organization to validate the value of the AI tool before adopting it across all projects. Testing on a pilot will help to identify errors, security risks, limitations, and possible resistance from the technical team. If the benefits are achieved without affecting the quality or the security, the organization can begin to extend the initiative to more projects.</p></td></tr>
</table>

<p>• What can you do to address the weaknesses of your organization&#x27;s culture? </p>

<table style="border-collapse:collapse;width:100%;">
<tr><td style="border:1px solid #b8b8b8;padding:8px;vertical-align:top;"><p>I think there are two principal root causes that could explain the organizational culture. The relationship between management and technical teams is determined by the important role of management in communicating with the customers. For this reason, technical employees could feel that every idea needs management approval or validation before being explored, as only management gets direct answers from the customers. Another cause is that experimentation and innovation are expected as part of the job but are rarely recognized. Because of this, employees will be more motivated to deliver the requested solution instead of proposing initiatives that would involve additional risk for them and the organization.</p><p>To address these weaknesses, the organization can create more opportunities for technical employees to participate in meetings with the customer, allowing them to not depend on management to provide validation. Management could also create spaces where employees can present their ideas without being negatively affected if the idea is not finally implemented, and praise the effort on improving the ways of working, even if the idea is not implemented.</p><p>When implementing my AI initiative, I would involve the technical team from the beginning, so senior engineers can help define the standards that AI-generated infrastructure and code must follow. This will reduce resistance because the employees will participate in the design of this new way of working instead of only receiving a new tool to use.</p></td></tr>
