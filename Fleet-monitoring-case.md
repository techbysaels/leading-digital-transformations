# From a centralized platform to specialized applications in fleet monitoring

I was part of a large project in a company that sought to change from a centralized monitoring platform that included logistics, monitoring, and maintenance modules to specialized applications for each of the previous modules they had. That decision was made by the board of directors of the company because of two principal reasons: technical teams detected new use cases that could be leveraged by using IoT sensors to obtain vehicle data (including predictive maintenance and fuel monitoring), and the previous platform was not able to process the increasing fleet of the customer (from 50 vehicles to 500 vehicles, increased in batches). In the end, the change was done successfully; however, there were many challenges to overcome.

## Strategic design lens

As the customer’s fleet expanded from 50 to 500 vehicles, the previous centralized platform became increasingly difficult to scale and also lacked some of the specialized capabilities required by the business. For that reason, a new architecture was proposed, separating logistics, monitoring, and maintenance into specialized applications while using an orchestrator to exchange valuable information between them. The solution introduced additional integration and maintenance complexity, but it provided what the business needed: scalability and evolution of functionalities.

The maintenance process also changed with this improvement. Previously, maintenance decisions depended on employees manually checking and entering vehicle mileage into the system, and when a limit was reached, the vehicle went to maintenance. With the new platform, vehicle data was collected automatically through IoT devices and could trigger maintenance work orders using predefined mileage rules or ML models for predictive maintenance. This reduced the repetitive task and allowed maintenance specialists to focus on evaluating and resolving actual maintenance issues.

For operational decision-making, the solution expanded the type of information available. The previous system relied only on GPS information and manually configured dangerous zones. The installation of new devices that could be connected to the vehicle computer to obtain more data, such as fuel-level sensors and panic buttons, enabled the organization to detect events like possible fuel theft or assaults and respond by identifying new risk areas. The historical information of the sensors and raised maintenance work orders also provided the data required to develop predictive maintenance models.

## Political lens

The implementation required the support of stakeholders who had influence over the project and the employees who would use the platform. One of the main stakeholders, the logistics manager, initially distrusted the effectiveness of the new solution and repeatedly expressed his concerns during meetings. His position influenced members of his team, creating more resistance toward the solution. Instead of relying only on explanations about the benefits of the solution, the project team used the testing rounds to demonstrate the capabilities of the platform with concrete results. With this strategy, the support of the logistics manager gradually changed, and with its influence on his team, began to regain the trust and facilitate greater acceptance of the changes.

Another situation that happened was when deciding how resources and responsibilities would be distributed among the applications, because the logistics and maintenance areas compete for priority in the development of their modules, as each considered their requirements were more critical. To address this, the project manager established a prioritization process based on business impact, operational risk, and dependencies. Then, in the prioritization meetings, both areas participated and agreed on the functionalities to be delivered first. In this way, the ownership of the transformation was shared between the parts instead of having the perception that one department should have greater influence than the other.

## Cultural lens

The employees had used the previous platform for years, developing routines and expectations around it. Even when the new applications offered greater capabilities, many employees still preferred the familiar system; however, the transition was necessary for the business. To facilitate the transition, key users were involved from the beginning of the implementation. Their participation allowed them to become familiar with the new platform, understand its functionality, and build trust in the solution. By the end of the project, these key users were able to help their colleagues and explain how the new system should be used, making the transition less disruptive.

Another challenge appeared in the maintenance team’s expectations regarding machine learning. Some workers initially expected the model to achieve 100% accuracy and had concerns about the automation replacing their jobs. The accuracy expectations were addressed by explaining that the model would achieve an 80% accuracy and that expert validation would still be necessary because the algorithm could not capture every environmental and operational condition affecting the vehicle. That explanation also helped them to understand that the model was intended to support their decisions rather than replace their professional judgement.

## Conclusion

The successful transition from a centralized platform to specialized applications resulted from the interaction of all three lenses. From the strategic design lens, the new solution solved scalability and functionality problems while introducing automation and predictive capabilities. From the political lens, the project team managed the influence of important stakeholders and built support with evidence and participation in the process. From the cultural lens, involving key users and addressing employees' expectations about AI helped the organization to gradually accept the new way of working. This case shows that technological transformation depends on more than selecting the appropriate solution. Successful change also needs to consider the interests and influence of stakeholders, operational needs to be addressed, and employee support, as they will be adapting their routines, expectations, and professional roles.
